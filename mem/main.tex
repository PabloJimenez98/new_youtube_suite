\abstractenglish{
Publishing a long-form video podcast on YouTube requires a series of repetitive post-production tasks (subtitles, chapters, short-form clips, descriptions) and a number of editorial decisions, such as which moments deserve a clip or whether a title and thumbnail will attract clicks, that are usually taken without quantitative support. Existing tools address these tasks in isolation, rely on paid cloud services, and do not learn from the creator's own performance data.

This Final Master's Project presents the design and development of \textit{YouTube Suite}, a self-hosted platform that turns a raw podcast recording into the artefacts YouTube expects, and that is designed to replace editorial guesswork progressively with models trained on the creator's own performance data. The platform integrates eight tools: transcription and subtitle generation (faster-whisper), speaker diarization (pyannote.audio), chapter generation with a locally trained boundary model, show-notes drafting with a local large language model, a relevance model distilled from heuristic and language-model pseudo-labels, an automatic Shorts generator, a title/thumbnail click-through-rate predictor, and a hook-strength scorer. A market-analysis pipeline collects public data from curated podcast channels and private analytics from the operator's own channel, and analyses them with statistical models. Finally, a 100-million-parameter language model was trained from scratch, including its tokeniser, and integrated as an alternative to the pretrained model.

The system runs entirely on consumer hardware without paid APIs. Development followed a milestone plan (M0--M16) with planning, development and testing sub-phases, and each trained model was compared with a simpler baseline on held-out data and promoted only if it passed a rule fixed before training. The trained chapter model doubles the boundary F1 of an unsupervised baseline in English at 3 and 5 seconds of tolerance and improves every metric in Spanish; the relevance model correlates better with its teacher than its heuristic baseline does (Spearman 0.652 against 0.545), met the pre-set blind-review threshold against the teacher (3 of 5 episodes), and scores an episode in one second instead of minutes; and the in-house language model passes the automatic gates for chapter titles but not for show notes or relevance. Real click-through and retention labels did not exist during the project, so the CTR and hook scorers remain transparent heuristics and their training is future work. The report describes the state of the art, the data-science methods of each component, the measured results and their limitations, and an economic evaluation of the self-hosted approach.
}

\section{Context and justification of the project}

Over the last decade, video-on-demand (VOD) platforms have become the main distribution channel for audiovisual content, and YouTube in particular has become one of the leading platforms for podcasts, which are increasingly recorded and published as video. Content discovery on these platforms is governed by recommendation systems that optimise engagement signals such as watch time and click-through rate (CTR) \cite{recommender_systems_streaming, vod_bigdata}. As a consequence, the success of an episode depends not only on its content but also on how it is packaged: subtitles, chapters, title, thumbnail, description and short-form clips that act as entry points to the full episode.

For a small production team, preparing this material is costly and repetitive. A one-hour episode requires a synchronised transcript, speaker attribution, chapter markers, several vertical clips, and a description with tags. Moreover, the decisions with the largest impact on reach, namely which moments are worth clipping and which title and thumbnail will be clicked, are usually taken by intuition. The platform provides the data needed to learn from past performance (public statistics through the YouTube Data API, retention curves through the Analytics API, and real thumbnail impressions and CTR through the Reporting API \cite{yt_data_api}), but this data is rarely connected back to the production process.

The commercial tools available in this space (automatic clipping, captioning or title-testing services) solve isolated steps, are offered as paid cloud services that require uploading unpublished material to third parties, and do not train on the creator's own results. Open-source projects cover the mechanical parts (transcription, cropping, caption burn-in) but, as the prior-art review in Chapter~\ref{ch:soa} shows, none of them trains a dedicated model to score the relevance of transcript segments.

This project addresses that gap with \textit{YouTube Suite}, an integrated platform for video-podcast producers built around three principles:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Integration}: a single workflow in which every tool reads from and writes to the same data model, so that artefacts produced by one tool (e.g.\ the transcript) are reused by all the others.
\item \textbf{Local execution}: all models, including the language models, run on the operator's own hardware, which keeps unpublished material private and avoids per-use costs.
\item \textbf{Data feedback}: a market-analysis pipeline accumulates real performance data that is used to train and progressively improve the platform's custom models.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Objectives of the project}
\label{s:objectives}

\subsection*{General objective}

To design, implement and validate a self-hosted platform that automates the post-production of YouTube video podcasts and supports editorial decisions with machine learning models trained on real performance data, running entirely on consumer hardware.

\subsection*{Specific objectives}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Build a modular back end (REST API, relational database and asynchronous job pipeline) in which each AI tool is an independent, traceable job.
\item Implement accurate transcription with word-level timestamps and downloadable subtitles (SRT/WebVTT), and speaker diarization integrated into the transcript and subtitles.
\item Develop a bilingual (English/Spanish) chapter generator based on models trained locally, and evaluate it against an unsupervised baseline with standard segmentation metrics.
\item Generate show notes (title suggestions, description, tags and summary) with a local language model and validated structured output.
\item Design a transcript-based relevance model and use it to generate vertical short-form clips automatically (boundary selection, subject-tracking crop and optional burned-in captions).
\item Build a market-analysis pipeline that collects public data from a curated list of podcast channels and private analytics (engagement, retention and real CTR) from the operator's own channel, within the API quota limits.
\item Implement a title/thumbnail CTR predictor and a hook-strength scorer, bootstrapped with heuristics and designed to be trained with ranking objectives as labelled data accumulates.
\item Provide a model versioning and retraining pipeline in which a trained model replaces its heuristic predecessor only after it demonstrably outperforms it.
\item Expose all functionality through a web interface, and verify every component with automated tests and a regression suite.
\item Evaluate the economic cost and viability of the self-hosted approach.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Impact on sustainability, ethical-social aspects and diversity}
\label{s:etic}

This project integrates the Competence of Ethical and Global Commitment (CCEG) following the transversal guidelines for Final Projects. The reflection is structured around the three required dimensions.

\subsection*{Sustainability}

The main environmental impact comes from the energy consumed by model inference and training. The design reduces it in several ways, aligned with SDG~9 (Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure) and SDG~12 (Responsible Consumption and Production):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reuse of artefacts.} Audio is extracted once and stored; the transcript is computed once and reused by diarization, chapters, show notes, relevance scoring and clip captions.
\item \textbf{Small, frozen models.} The chapter and relevance models are lightweight heads trained on top of frozen pretrained encoders, and their trainings last minutes to hours on a single consumer GPU instead of large-scale clusters. The one exception is the in-house language model, whose training took about 100 GPU hours (Section~\ref{s:economic}).
\item \textbf{Consumer hardware.} The platform runs on a single desktop computer; model sizes and quantisation are chosen to fit it rather than to require server equipment.
\item \textbf{Efficient data collection.} Public data is collected with batched API calls and free RSS feeds, avoiding continuous polling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Ethical behaviour and social responsibility}

Channel statistics and analytics are collected through the official YouTube APIs: public data from channels, and private analytics from the operator's own channel under OAuth consent, with credentials encrypted at rest. The training data for the chapter models (VidChapters-7M \cite{yang2023vidchapters}) is derived from public YouTube videos whose rights remain with their authors; it is used only to train models for internal use and is never redistributed. Processing everything locally means that unpublished episodes and guests' voices are never sent to third-party services.

The main ethical risk is the optimisation of content purely for engagement metrics, and the production of decontextualised clips. The platform mitigates this in two ways: it never publishes anything by itself (every artefact is reviewed and published manually by the operator), and its scoring models expose interpretable outputs (e.g.\ the feature contributions of the hook scorer) instead of opaque rankings. This supports SDG~8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth) by reducing repetitive work for small creators while keeping editorial control in human hands.

\subsection*{Diversity, gender and human rights}

Speech recognition, diarization and language models may perform unevenly across accents, languages and voices because of biases in their training data. Two measures address this: the chapter models are explicitly evaluated on a held-out Spanish test set in addition to English, and speaker labels are anonymous (\texttt{SPEAKER\_00}, \texttt{SPEAKER\_01}, \dots), so no identity or gender is inferred from a voice. On the positive side, automatic subtitles improve accessibility for deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers, and bilingual support for Spanish, a language underrepresented in public chaptering datasets, contributes to SDG~10 (Reduced Inequalities). For the same reason the relevance model uses a multilingual encoder, and the language model trained in the project uses 70\% Spanish text.

\section{Approach and method followed}

The project followed a plan-driven incremental methodology in two stages.

\textbf{Design stage.} Four phases preceded any implementation: (i)~\textit{scope definition}, in which each candidate tool was discussed and classified as in or out of scope for v1, and as custom or external; (ii)~\textit{research}, a review of prior art, datasets and API constraints for each component; (iii)~\textit{specification}, a full technical description of inputs, outputs, internal steps, data model and model architectures; and (iv)~\textit{development planning}, the decomposition of the work into sixteen milestones (M0--M15). A seventeenth milestone (M16, an in-house language model trained from scratch) was added in November, once the others were done.

\textbf{Development stage.} Milestones are ordered so that the simplest end-to-end path (upload $\rightarrow$ job $\rightarrow$ visible result) is working first, followed by the tool suite, the market-analysis pipeline and, finally, the custom-model training loop that depends on data accumulated by the earlier milestones. Each milestone has three sub-phases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Planning}: a design and test-plan note is written before any code. Architectural choices are recorded as dated \textit{decision records} with context, decision, rejected alternatives and consequences.
\item \textbf{Development}: only the milestone's scope is implemented.
\item \textbf{Testing}: new tests are written and the whole existing suite is re-run as a regression check; a milestone is not closed until both pass, together with a manual end-to-end check on a real episode.
\end{enumerate}

Two practices were added to make technical decisions evidence-based. First, \textit{measured spikes}: whenever a decision depended on hardware behaviour (GPU memory, speed), a short throwaway experiment measured it before the design was committed. Second, model development is carried out as a documented \textit{experiment}, with numbered notebooks committed with their outputs, a tested shared library, per-run configuration and metrics files, and a dated log, so that the path from data to the exported model can be followed and reproduced.

\section{Brief summary of the products obtained}

\textbf{YouTube Suite platform.} A monorepo containing a FastAPI back end, an arq worker, a PostgreSQL schema managed with Alembic migrations, and an Angular single-page application, deployable on a single machine with Docker Compose for the database and the queue broker.

\textbf{Speech processing tools.} GPU transcription with word-level timestamps and SRT/WebVTT export, and speaker diarization with word-level speaker assignment and speaker-aware subtitles.

\textbf{Content generation tools.} A bilingual chapter generator based on a locally trained boundary model, a show-notes generator based on a local language model, and an automatic Shorts generator driven by a relevance model.

\textbf{Market-analysis pipeline.} Scheduled collection of public data (YouTube Data API~v3 and RSS feeds) and private analytics of the operator's channel (Analytics API~v2 and Reporting API~v1), with precomputed statistical analyses of the market.

\textbf{Custom models and training pipeline.} A trained relevance model, heuristic CTR and hook scorers designed to be learnt later, a model versioning and promotion pipeline, and reproducible experiments (notebooks, tested libraries and exported model bundles).

\textbf{In-house language model.} A 100-million-parameter Transformer trained from scratch (tokeniser, pretraining and post-training), exported to the same runtime as the pretrained model and selectable in the application.

\textbf{Documentation.} Specification, development plan, decision records and research notes that trace every design decision.

\chapter*{Glossary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Glossary}

\begin{description}
\item[Ablation] An experiment that changes one element of a recipe and keeps the rest equal, to measure the effect of that element.
\item[API] Application Programming Interface. A set of protocols enabling communication between software components.
\item[ASR] Automatic Speech Recognition. The task of converting spoken audio to text.
\item[bf16] Brain floating point. A 16-bit number format with the range of 32-bit floats, used to train neural networks faster and with less memory.
\item[BiLSTM] Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory. A recurrent network that processes a sequence in both directions.
\item[Blind review] A comparison in which the reviewer does not know which model produced each output.
\item[BPE] Byte-Pair Encoding. A method that learns a vocabulary of sub-word tokens from a corpus.
\item[bpb] Bits per byte. The average number of bits a language model needs to encode held-out text, per byte; lower is better and it does not depend on the tokeniser.
\item[CIDEr] Consensus-based Image Description Evaluation. A metric that compares generated text with references using TF-IDF-weighted n-grams.
\item[CTR] Click-Through Rate. The ratio of clicks to thumbnail impressions.
\item[DER] Diarization Error Rate. The fraction of time that is missed, falsely detected as speech or assigned to the wrong speaker.
\item[Diarization] The task of determining which speaker is active at each moment of a recording.
\item[Distillation] Training a model (the student) to reproduce the outputs of another model (the teacher).
\item[DPO] Direct Preference Optimisation. Training on pairs of preferred and rejected answers so that the preferred ones become more likely.
\item[EMA] Exponential Moving Average. A smoothing in which each new value is mixed with a fixed fraction of the previous smoothed value.
\item[F1@5s] F1 score of predicted boundaries, counting a prediction as correct if it is within 5 seconds of a reference boundary.
\item[GGUF] File format for (optionally quantised) language models used by llama.cpp.
\item[GPU] Graphics Processing Unit. A processor suited to parallel computation, used here for model inference and training.
\item[GQA] Grouped-Query Attention. An attention layer in which several query heads share one key/value head, which reduces memory.
\item[JWT] JSON Web Token. A signed token used for stateless authentication.
\item[LID] Language Identification. Detecting the language of a text or of an audio signal.
\item[LLM] Large Language Model. A neural language model trained on large text corpora to predict the next token.
\item[Muon] An optimiser that orthogonalises the update of each weight matrix before applying it.
\item[OAuth] An authorisation protocol that grants an application limited access to a user's account.
\item[ORM] Object-Relational Mapping. A technique for translating between database relations and programming language objects.
\item[PCA] Principal Component Analysis. A projection of data onto the directions of largest variance, used here to draw a two-dimensional map.
\item[$P_k$] Probabilistic segmentation error metric: the probability that two positions at a fixed distance are wrongly classified as belonging to the same or different segments.
\item[Pretraining] Training a language model from random weights to predict the next token of large amounts of text.
\item[Pseudo-label] A label produced by a model or a rule instead of a human, used as a training target.
\item[QK-norm] Normalisation of the queries and keys of an attention layer before their dot product.
\item[Quantisation] Representation of model weights with fewer bits (e.g.\ 8 or 4) to reduce memory and computation.
\item[ReLU$^2$] An activation function that squares the output of a rectified linear unit.
\item[RMSNorm] Root-Mean-Square normalisation. A layer normalisation that only rescales activations by their root mean square.
\item[RoPE] Rotary Position Embedding. A way to encode token positions by rotating the queries and keys of attention.
\item[SFT] Supervised Fine-Tuning. Continued training on example prompts and answers, with the loss computed on the answers.
\item[SHAP] SHapley Additive exPlanations. A method that attributes a prediction to its input features.
\item[Silhouette] A score between $-1$ and 1 that measures how well each point fits its cluster compared with the nearest other cluster.
\item[Spearman] Rank correlation coefficient: whether two lists of scores order the items in the same way (1 same order, 0 unrelated).
\item[SRT] SubRip Text. A subtitle file format containing timestamped text segments.
\item[SwiGLU] A gated feed-forward layer in which one linear projection, passed through the Swish function, multiplies another.
\item[TF-IDF] Term Frequency--Inverse Document Frequency. A weight that is high for words frequent in one document and rare in the others.
\item[tIoU-F1] Boundary F1 averaged over several temporal-overlap (IoU) thresholds between predicted and reference segments.
\item[VAD] Voice Activity Detection. Detection of the parts of an audio signal that contain speech.
\item[VOD] Video-on-Demand. A media distribution model allowing users to access video content on request.
\item[VRAM] Video RAM. The memory of the GPU.
\item[WebVTT] Web Video Text Tracks. The W3C subtitle format used by HTML5 video.
\item[WER] Word Error Rate. The proportion of word substitutions, deletions and insertions in a transcript relative to a reference.
\item[WSD] Warmup--Stable--Decay. A learning-rate schedule with a short warm-up, a long constant phase and a final decay.
\end{description}
